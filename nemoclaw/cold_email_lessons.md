# Cold Email Lessons for Nemoclaw

 Adapted from Alex Hormozi's framework for our AI receptionist context.

---

## Lesson 1: The Give-Give-Ask Sequence

**Hormozi principle:** Two value deposits before any ask. Never pitch in the first email.

**Why it matters for home services:** Pest control, HVAC, and lawn care shops get hit with naked sales pitches all day. A value-first approach cuts through the noise and earns a reply.

**Example — Email 1 (Give):**
> "Most pest control companies we work with are losing 30–40% of their inbound calls to voicemail because staff is in the field. We built a simple voicemail-to-SMS alert that gets the tech moving in minutes instead of hours. Happy to share the setup."

**Example — Email 2 (Give):**
> "Following up on the voicemail setup — we just helped a shop in Tampa cut their call drop rate by half in 30 days using the same approach. Here's the breakdown of what we did."

**Example — Email 3 (Ask):**
> "Worth a quick 10-min call to show you how this could work for your route? No pitch, just a walkthrough of what's been working for shops like yours."

## Lesson 2: The 100 Rule for Volume

**Hormozi principle:** 100 cold emails/day minimum. Volume creates statistical predictability.

**Why it matters:** At 1% reply rate, 100 emails = 1 conversation. At scale, that math funds a pipeline.

**For Nemoclaw:** The AI receptionist handles the follow-ups and sequence execution. Humans set the direction. The machine handles the volume.

**Daily target:** 50–100 cold emails/day to pest control shops in Tampa first, then expand.

---

## Lesson 3: The Value Test

**Hormozi principle:** Every email must leave the recipient better off having received it—even if they never reply.

**Why it matters:** Self-interested copy gets deleted, marked spam, or reported. Value-first copy builds reputation and earns replies even from people who don't convert.

**Application:** Before sending any email, ask: "Is the recipient better off having read this?" If no, rewrite or don't send.

---

## Lesson 4: Rejection Is Data

**Hormozi principle:** A "no" means one of three things — timing wrong, offer doesn't match priority, or wrong person. Never take it personally.

**Why it matters for cold outreach:** Half of your prospects will say no initially but become buyers 6–18 months later. Follow-up with new value, not pressure.

**Nemoclaw adaptation:**
- Silence → follow up in 14 days with a different value drop
- "Not interested" → wait 60–90 days, reframe around a new problem
- Wrong person → update targeting criteria, don't recycle the list

---

## Lesson 5: Subject Lines Don't Matter If the Copy Is Bad

**Hormozi principle:** Fix volume before fixing subject lines. No subject line optimization saves a weak offer or low-volume campaign.

**Why it matters:** Spending 3 hours on the "perfect subject line" while sending 20 emails/day is backwards. Get to 100/day first, then optimize.

**Sequence for Nemoclaw:**
1. First: build the email sequence (Give-Give-Ask)
2. Second: set volume target (50–100/day)
3. Third: test subject line variants

---

## Lesson 6: Track the Right Metrics

**Hormozi principle:** Leading indicators (volume + reply rate + call booking) daily. Lagging indicators (close rate + revenue) monthly.

**Nemoclaw KPI stack:**

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Emails sent/day | 50–100 |
| Reply rate | >1% |
| Call booking rate | >10% of replies |
| Show rate | >70% |
| Close rate | measure, don't target |

---

## Lesson 7: The Follow-Up Loop Is Where Deals Are Made

**Hormozi principle:** Most sales happen after the 5th–7th touch. Most reps quit after the 2nd.

**Why it matters:** Your first email is rarely enough. The money is in the sequence.

**Nemoclaw sequence cadence:**
- Email 1: Day 0 — First give (resource)
- Email 2: Day 3–7 — Second give (case study or deeper insight)
- Email 3: Day 10–14 — Ask (call request)
- Email 4: Day 21 — Follow-up with new value (no ask)
- Email 5: Day 45 — Re-engagement with new data or offer
- Email 6: Day 90 — Final check-in

## What to Avoid

- ❌ Leading with a pitch in the first email
- ❌ Using "just checking in" as a follow-up (adds no value)
- ❌ Sending the same pitch repeatedly after a "no"
- ❌ Caring more about subject lines than volume
- ❌ Quitting after 1–2 touches
