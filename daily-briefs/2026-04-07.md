# Daily AI Brief - 2026-04-07

## CEO brief

1. **Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 4.6 today, raising the bar for long-horizon enterprise work.**
   Anthropic says Opus 4.6 improves coding, agentic task persistence, debugging, research, finance workflows, and introduces a **1M-token context window in beta** while keeping API pricing unchanged at **$5/$25 per million tokens**. This matters because the differentiation is shifting from chat quality to durable multi-step work across large codebases and business documents. **Action:** if we rely on AI for internal ops, product analytics, or code review, benchmark Opus 4.6 immediately against our current default for high-value workflows. *(Source: Anthropic, Apr 7, 2026)*

2. **Anthropic also acquired Vercept, signaling that “computer use” is becoming a strategic moat, not a side feature.**
   The company framed the deal around improving Claude’s ability to perceive and act inside live software, and cited Sonnet 4.6 reaching **72.5% on OSWorld**, approaching human-level performance on spreadsheet and browser tasks. This is strategically important: the next margin layer may come from owning end-to-end task execution inside enterprise applications, not just model inference. **Action:** prioritize vendors with credible browser/app control and workflow reliability, and treat UI-level automation as a near-term product surface. *(Source: Anthropic, Apr 7, 2026)*

3. **Microsoft started deploying its Maia 200 AI chip today and says it will host OpenAI’s GPT-5.2 on it.**
   According to The Verge, Maia 200 is built on **TSMC 3nm**, has **100B+ transistors**, claims **3x FP4 performance vs Amazon Trainium3**, and delivers **30% better performance per dollar** than Microsoft’s latest deployed fleet hardware. This signals that hyperscaler economics are tightening around custom silicon, with direct implications for margin, pricing power, and model distribution leverage. **Action:** assume frontier model pricing and availability will increasingly be shaped by cloud-chip alliances; avoid single-cloud lock-in where possible. *(Source: The Verge reporting Microsoft announcement, Apr 7, 2026)*

4. **Google’s video stack is getting cheaper and more productizable, which lowers the bar for AI-native media features.**
   Google says **Veo 3.1 Lite** is now available in the Gemini API as its most cost-effective video model, at **less than 50% of the cost of Veo 3.1 Fast**, with support for text-to-video and image-to-video, 720p/1080p, and 4s/6s/8s durations. Google also said it would **cut Veo 3.1 Fast pricing on April 7**. **Action:** if any portfolio company touches ads, commerce, creator tools, training, or customer support, revisit whether short-form video generation just crossed from demo to shippable unit economics. *(Source: Google, Apr 7 pricing update / Mar 31 post)*

5. **OpenAI’s policy agenda is expanding from products into industrial policy, which means regulation and capital allocation are becoming board-level AI issues.**
   TechCrunch reports OpenAI published proposals including **public wealth funds, robot taxes, four-day workweek subsidies, portable benefits, infrastructure subsidies, and treating AI more like a utility**. Regardless of whether these specific proposals land, the signal is clear: major AI labs are now actively trying to shape labor, tax, energy, and industrial policy. **Action:** track AI exposure not just through product and vendor strategy, but also through regulation, power availability, and tax policy in core markets. *(Source: TechCrunch on OpenAI policy paper, Apr 6, 2026)*
