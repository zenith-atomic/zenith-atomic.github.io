# Nicolas pre-market brief, 2026-04-18

- **Commodities:** Oil is the main move, Brent/WTI sold off hard after Iran said the Strait of Hormuz is open to commercial traffic during the ceasefire window; gold should stay supported by the same risk backdrop, but the bigger market read is lower immediate supply shock risk. That matters because energy is the cleanest transmission channel into inflation, margins, and rate-cut odds. **Source:** Reuters, Apr. 17-18, 2026 (Hormuz / oil updates).

- **Tech:** No fresh tech-specific catalyst overnight. The last notable tape was the Nasdaq pushing to a record on Apr. 15 as investors rotated back into megacap tech, so today is more about whether lower oil and calmer macro help risk appetite than about a new tech story. **Source:** Reuters, Apr. 15, 2026, Nasdaq record high.

- **Treasury policy / rates / yields:** The Fed is still on hold from the March 18 FOMC, with policy unchanged and the next meeting not until Apr. 28-29; markets are still trading around when cuts can resume, and that is very sensitive to energy-driven inflation swings. Lower oil helps the bond market narrative, while higher oil would push yields and rate-cut expectations the other way. **Source:** Federal Reserve, March 18, 2026 FOMC statement / minutes; Reuters, Apr. 17, 2026, oil drop and Fed-cut repricing.

- **Geopolitics:** Meaningful event. Iran’s Hormuz messaging remains the key geopolitical input, and any reversal, tanker incident, or renewed blockade threat would immediately hit energy, shipping, and inflation expectations. For markets, this is a live tail-risk, not background noise. **Source:** Reuters / Reuters Connect, Apr. 17-18, 2026, Hormuz updates.

**CEO takeaway:** Risk is easing if Hormuz stays open, which argues for staying patient on energy-heavy hedges, but keeping dry powder for any headline reversal before the open.
