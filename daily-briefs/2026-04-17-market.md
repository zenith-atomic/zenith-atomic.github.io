# Pre-Market Market Conditions Brief
**Date:** 2026-04-17 (America/New_York)

- **Commodities:** Oil remains the key commodity stress point. In its April 2026 Oil Market Report, the IEA said Middle East disruption has cut overall oil exports by more than 13 mb/d, drove a March global stock draw of 85 mb, and is expected to push April global demand down 2.3 mb/d year over year, with physical crude prices near $150/bbl. **Why it matters:** this is still an inflation and margin story first, especially for transport, industrials, chemicals, and consumer businesses exposed to freight or fuel. **Source:** IEA, Oil Market Report - April 2026, https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-april-2026

- **Tech:** TSMC’s Q1 results were a clean positive read-through for AI and advanced-node demand. The company reported NT$361.6 billion in net income, up 60.3% year over year, and guided Q2 revenue to $28.4 billion to $29.2 billion, with 2026 capital spending unchanged at $38 billion to $42 billion. **Why it matters:** that keeps the AI supply chain growth signal intact before the U.S. open and supports the case that leading-edge semiconductor demand is still outrunning the broader cycle. **Source:** TSMC Q1 2026 results, https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-04/8d5f6d0d2bd4ca6ad46f620e2069147a8f3d8ea6/1Q26Presentation_E.pdf

- **Treasury policy / rates / yields:** No fresh Fed or Treasury policy surprise this morning, but yields pushed higher into the close Thursday. Treasury’s daily curve shows the 2-year at 3.80% from 3.79% on April 15, the 10-year at 4.32% from 4.29%, and the 30-year at 4.93% from 4.89%. **Why it matters:** the move tightens financial conditions at the margin, raises discount rates for long-duration equities, and keeps financing costs elevated even without a new policy headline. **Source:** U.S. Treasury daily yield curve, https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?field_tdr_date_value=2026&type=daily_treasury_yield_curve

- **Geopolitics:** This is still a real macro variable, not background noise. Even after the announced ceasefire, shipping normalisation through Hormuz is not settled, and Reuters reported market participants were still waiting for clarity as tankers remained backed up in the Gulf. **Why it matters:** if flows do not normalize quickly, energy, freight, and insurance costs can stay elevated and keep cross-asset risk sentiment fragile. **Source:** Reuters via Investing.com, https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/oil-prices-extend-losses-on-usiran-talks-and-demand-worries-4154156

**CEO takeaway:** Go into the open assuming energy and rates are still the two macro risk levers. The opportunity is selective upside in AI-linked tech where demand signals remain firm, but positioning should stay disciplined because a fragile Hormuz reset and higher Treasury yields can tighten conditions fast.
